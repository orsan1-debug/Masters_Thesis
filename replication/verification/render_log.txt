  title: 'Balancing weights against maximum likelihood propensity scores: a simulation study of `balnet` across sample size, overlap, dimension and penalty choice'
  subtitle: 'ETC5860 Masters thesis, Monash University. Supervisor: Erik Sverdrup'
  author: Otis Sandford
  date: '2026-10-16'
  abstract: |
    Maximum likelihood fits propensity scores to predict treatment, balancing weights to balance covariates directly.
    `balnet` fits balancing weights by a lasso-penalised balancing loss whose path runs to exact balance or stops short where infeasible.
    We compare `balnet` with cross-validated lasso MLE weights, alone and augmented, by simulation across sample size, overlap, dimension past $p = n$, confounder density and outcome signal, with and without misspecification.
    Balancing weights have lower RMSE throughout, by a third to two thirds on average, except noisy outcomes and the poorest overlap.
    Whether the path reaches exact balance decides what else helps.
    Augmentation adds nothing there and restores the lead where it stops short.
    A second study finds stopping inside the path lowers RMSE for noisy outcomes or, once augmented, limited overlap.
    Cross-validating the balancing loss captures this most often, though none is best in more than three cells in ten.
    We recommend augmented balancing weights tuned this way.
  
    *Keywords:* balancing weights; propensity score; inverse probability weighting; lasso; Monte Carlo simulation.
